# Culture and Working Values

## Customer Clarity

Sampurna expects teams to distinguish confirmed facts from estimates. Current stock, a valid price, and a carrier scan are facts from operational systems. A replenishment date, allocation target, or delivery estimate remains provisional until the relevant event occurs.

## Ownership

The first team receiving a request should capture essential identifiers and route it correctly rather than asking the buyer to repeat the full story. Ownership does not mean solving issues outside one's authority; it means maintaining a clear case owner and next action.

## Data Discipline

Stable identifiers are preferred over names. Staff use buyer ID, SKU, order ID, shipment ID or tracking number, invoice reference, warehouse ID, and authorization number. Descriptions help people read a case but do not replace keys.

## Safety and Quality

Safety concerns take priority over sales and service speed. Suspected contamination, tampering, chemical exposure, batch failure, or unsafe storage is escalated immediately. No employee should return suspect goods to saleable inventory simply to close a discrepancy.

## Commercial Integrity

Prices, discounts, tiers, credit limits, and policy exceptions require appropriate approval and system records. Teams should not promise undocumented concessions or alter historical records to match a later agreement.

## Respectful Service

Support communication should be concise, factual, and respectful. Staff should avoid blame while an investigation is open. Customers and retailers receive the reason for a decision and the next available step, subject to confidentiality and legal limits.

## Continuous Improvement

Repeated stockouts, claims, late deliveries, pricing disputes, and data defects should be analysed for root cause. A case can be resolved for the buyer and still require a preventive action for the business.
