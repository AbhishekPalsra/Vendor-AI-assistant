# Business Continuity and Service Disruption

## Purpose

This guide covers operational response to severe weather, warehouse closure, carrier outage, system failure, labour disruption, infrastructure loss, or another event affecting fulfilment.

## Priorities

1. Protect people and product safety.
2. Preserve accurate inventory, order, and payment records.
3. Identify affected warehouses, routes, buyers, and orders.
4. Stop incorrect promises or duplicate processing.
5. Restore critical service and communicate reliable updates.

## Warehouse Disruption

When a hub cannot operate, new allocation may be paused. Orders can be rerouted only after alternate stock, route, capacity, price, tax, and delivery implications are checked. In-transit transfers do not become instantly available.

## Carrier Disruption

Logistics evaluates alternative carriers and methods. Changing a carrier may change tracking numbers, transit estimates, freight charges, or destination coverage. Buyers should be notified when the change materially affects service.

## System Disruption

If inventory, pricing, buyer, or order systems are unavailable, teams should avoid creating untraceable commitments. Approved offline procedures must preserve identifiers, timestamps, approvals, and later reconciliation.

## Communication

Updates should state affected service, geography, known start time, actions underway, buyer impact, next update time, and whether estimates are reliable. Avoid declaring full recovery until backlog and data reconciliation are checked.

## Recovery

Recovery includes processing backlog, verifying reservations, preventing duplicate shipment, reconciling payments, checking carrier events, and reviewing product condition after environmental disruption.

## Post-Incident Review

Material incidents should document timeline, impact, root causes, decisions, controls that worked or failed, corrective actions, owners, and due dates.
