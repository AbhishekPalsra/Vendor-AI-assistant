# Delivery Proof and Exceptions

## Proof of Delivery

Proof of delivery may include recipient name, delivery timestamp, signature, one-time password confirmation, photograph, geolocation event, or a carrier scan. Available evidence varies by carrier and route.

## Receiver Responsibilities

The receiver should check carton count and visible condition before acknowledging delivery. Visible shortage, crushed cartons, leakage, wet packaging, or tampering should be recorded on the delivery document whenever possible.

## Common Exceptions

### Recipient Unavailable

The carrier may make another attempt. Repeated failure can lead to return-to-origin and additional freight charges.

### Address Incorrect or Incomplete

Support should obtain corrected address details. A changed postal code may alter serviceability, carrier, charge, and estimated delivery date.

### Delivery Refused

The reason must be recorded. Unjustified refusal can result in return freight, restocking charges, or account review.

### Partial Delivery

Record received and missing carton counts. Determine whether the order was intentionally split before opening a shortage claim.

### Marked Delivered but Not Received

Confirm the delivery address, receiver, timestamp, neighbouring reception points, and proof-of-delivery evidence. Escalate to Logistics with the tracking number.

## Damage at Delivery

Visible damage should be photographed before unpacking when safe. The buyer should preserve the carton, label, product, and delivery receipt and report within 48 hours.

## Estimated Versus Actual Dates

Expected delivery is an estimate. Delivery date is an event record. Systems and assistants must not treat an expected date as proof that the shipment arrived.
