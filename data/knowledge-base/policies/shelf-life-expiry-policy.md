# Shelf-Life and Expiry Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how dated goods are received, stored, allocated, and reviewed. Manufacturer labels and product-specific agreements take priority.

## Receiving

Receiving teams should check expiry or best-before dates when present and confirm that remaining shelf life meets internal and buyer-specific thresholds. Unreadable, altered, or missing date codes require quarantine.

## Stock Rotation

First-Expiry, First-Out is the default method. The oldest acceptable batch is allocated first. A batch may be skipped when it is on hold, damaged, below the buyer's threshold, or subject to a quality investigation.

## Short-Dated Goods

Short-dated goods may be sold only through an approved process that states remaining shelf life, price treatment, return restrictions, and buyer acceptance. They must not be represented as standard stock when special conditions apply.

## Retailer Agreements

Some retailers may require a minimum percentage or number of days of shelf life at delivery. The specific written account or order agreement overrides a general threshold.

## Expired Goods

Expired goods are not saleable inventory. They must be segregated, blocked from allocation, and handled through approved supplier return or disposal procedures.

## Claims

A buyer reporting short-dated or expired stock should provide buyer ID, order ID, SKU, quantity, batch, date code, delivery date, and photographs. Reports of stock received below an agreed threshold should be made within 3 business days.

## Data Records

Where batch-level data exists, expiry should remain linked to the batch and warehouse. A product-level description alone is insufficient because the same SKU can have multiple batches.

## Safety Boundary

Do not advise a buyer to consume or use an expired product. Product-safety questions beyond label guidance require manufacturer or qualified authority input.
